## DETAILED COMPLIANCE FINDINGS

### 1. **2026 TCPA POSTURE FOR RINGLESS VOICEMAIL**

**Summary:** RVMs are definitively "calls" under TCPA as of November 2022 FCC Declaratory Ruling. No reversal. Status is unchanged through April 2026.

**Key Ruling:**
- [FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 22-85 (November 21, 2022)](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-22-85A1.pdf): RVMs to wireless phones are "calls" made using prerecorded voice and subject to TCPA. Prior express written consent required.
- [All About the Message LLC petition](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2022/12/14/2022-26673/telephone-consumer-protection-act-of-1991-petition-for-declaratory-ruling-of-all-about-the-message) was denied. AATM's argument (that RVMs are not "calls") was rejected despite significant industry lobbying.

**Critical Evolution (2025-2026):**
- [FCC One-to-One Consent Rule (effective Jan 2025)](https://blog.clickpointsoftware.com/2025-guide-to-tcpa-one-to-one-consent-can-spam-state-regulations): Consent is now **seller-specific, not just lead-generator generic**. The consumer must have been shown a clear disclosure naming **FindA.Sale specifically** at the time they agreed to RVM contact.
- [April 2025 FCC Rules on Opt-Out](https://leadcompliant.com/articles/tcpa-compliance/tcpa-ringless-voicemail-rules-2026): Consumers can revoke consent by any reasonable method (text STOP, email, phone call). Opt-out must be honored within 30 days.
- [April 2026 "Revoke All" Rule (delayed implementation)](https://blog.clickpointsoftware.com/tcpa-one-to-one-consent-can-spam-state-regulations): Treating an opt-out for one robocall type as opt-out for ALL robocalls from that caller. NOT YET IN EFFECT, but flagging for Phase 2+ planning.

**Enforcement Reality:**
- [FCC/FTC actively enforcing](https://www.dropcowboy.com/compliance/tcpa): Penalties $500–$1,500 per RVM sent in violation.
- [Litigation risk is real](https://tcpaworld.com/2022/11/21/rvp-officially-rip-fcc-determines-ringless-voicemails-are-subject-to-the-tcpa/): Private right of action under TCPA means class-action litigation is possible if consent/opt-out logs are insufficient.

**Finding:** RVM is **NOT a loophole**. It is a regulated channel with strict consent and opt-out obligations. No legal ambiguity exists as of April 2026.

**Risk Level:** ✅ **MEDIUM** — Compliance is achievable with proper documentation, but non-compliance is actively prosecuted.

### 2. **B2B vs B2C TCPA DISTINCTION**

**Summary:** B2B to business lines is NOT exempt from TCPA, but has lower regulatory risk than B2C.

**Key Facts:**
- [Business landlines ARE exempt from DNC rules](https://www.dnc.com/faq/are-b2b-calls-exempt-tcpa-regulations): If you call a business desk phone (published business number), no DNC check required.
- [Business cell phones (mobile) are NOT exempt](https://skipcall.io/en/blog/cold-calling-cell-phones): Even a CEO's personal mobile is treated as "residential" under TCPA, regardless of business use. Ninth Circuit precedent: mixed-use phones are presumed primarily residential.
- [Federal DNC exception](https://www.dnc.com/dnc-tcpa-guides-and-checklists/risks-b2b-under-tcpa) applies only to calls between businesses marketing B2B goods and services (excludes nondurable office/cleaning supplies).

**Application to this Campaign:**
- Estate sale operators likely publish a business phone number (landline or mobile registered to business).
- **Assumption needed from Patrick:** Are these numbers registered as business lines or personal mobile numbers?
- **If business landlines only:** DNC scrubbing is NOT required by federal law. State DNC lists still apply.
- **If business mobile numbers:** DNC scrubbing IS required (federal + state). Consent still required under TCPA regardless of exemption.

**Enforcement Delta:**
- [B2B enforcement is lower priority](https://www.kazlg.com/does-the-tcpa-apply-to-businesses/) than B2C, but FTC/FCC still pursue B2B RVM cases.
- FTC's stated focus (2024–2026): consumer harm. B2B targeting of established operators poses lower consumer-deception risk than targeting consumers directly.

**Finding:** Your B2B posture is defensible IF you can prove the phone list is business numbers. Consumer-registered mobile numbers targeting the same individuals personally = much higher risk.

**Risk Level:** ✅ **MEDIUM-to-HIGH** (depends on list source validation)

### 8. **PERSONA MISREPRESENTATION & DECEPTIVE PRACTICES**

**Summary:** The "Jen Martinez" persona in Version C raises FTC deceptive-practice risk.

**Script Analysis:**

> "If you're tagging inventory at eleven at night on a Friday, your system is broken. [pause] I know that feeling. I spent three years doing it."

**The Risk:**

This is a fictional persona (not the actual voice talent's real experience) presented as personal testimony. FTC guidance (2024–2026) treats this as potentially deceptive under multiple theories:

1. **Persona-as-Endorsement Rule:**
   - If a reasonable consumer believes "Jen" is a real person/real customer, the statement "I spent three years doing it" is an implied endorsement.
   - [FTC Endorsements & Testimonials Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P204500%20Guides%20Concerning%20Endors%20and%20Testimonials.pdf): endorsements must be truthful and represent actual experience of the endorser.
   - A fictional persona presented as real = potential deception.

2. **Operation AI Comply (2024–2026):**
   - [FTC's "Operation AI Comply"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/09/operation-ai-comply-continuing-crackdown-overpromises-ai-related-lies) targets businesses that misrepresent authenticity, credibility, or expertise.
   - While Version C uses a **human voice talent** (not AI voice), portraying a fictional work history as real is a parallel deceptive practice.
   - FTC's enforcement extends to "any use that could mislead reasonable consumers about who is speaking."

3. **Testimonial Substantiation:**
   - If "Jen's" 3-year experience is fictional, it cannot serve as substantiation for the product claim (that the system reduces prep time).
   - FTC requires testimonials to reflect typical results OR clearly disclose atypicality.

**Mitigating Factors:**
- ✅ Voice is human (not AI), reducing "deepfake" risk.
- ✅ No claim is made that "Jen" is a customer; she's introduced as a person relating to the pain.
- ❌ The implication remains: "I (Jen) spent 3 years doing this" suggests real personal experience.

**Safe Alternative Language:**
Instead of "I know that feeling. I spent three years doing it," you could:
- "We've talked to dozens of operators who described this exact frustration."
- "Most of our clients spent 30-50 hours on prep before switching."
- "[Human name], an operator in Grand Rapids, told us, '[quote about actual experience]'" — but only if that operator consents and the quote is real.

**Finding:** Current persona phrasing is at-risk under FTC deceptive-practice rules. Attorney review required.

**Risk Level:** ⚠️ **MEDIUM-HIGH** — Deceptive-practice risk is material but fixable with language revision.

### 10. **SPECIFICITY CLAIMS & SUBSTANTIATION**

**Summary:** "Cut prep time from 40 hours to under 15" is a quantifiable claim requiring FTC substantiation.

**FTC Substantiation Standard ([FTC Policy Statement](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-regarding-advertising-substantiation)):**

| Claim Type | Required Evidence | Your Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General** | "Reasonable basis" (varies by claim) | "Cuts prep time" | Assumes reasonable evidence |
| **Express** | Level claimed (e.g., "studies show") | "40 to 15 hours" | Express quantified claim = **higher burden** |
| **Testimonial** | Actual customer experience + typical results or clear disclosure if atypical | "One of our operators" | Must prove operator achieved these results |
| **Serious/Health Claims** | Randomized, double-blind clinical trial | N/A — not applicable | N/A |

**Your Evidence Requirement:**

For the claim "cut prep time from 40 hours to under 15," you must have:
1. **A real customer who used the product** and measured prep time before/after.
2. **Documented results** (logs, timestamps, or customer attestation).
3. **Confirmation this is typical** OR **clear disclosure if it's atypical** ("One exceptional customer achieved…").

**If Evidence Does Not Exist:**
- You cannot make the specific claim without substantiation.
- Softer claim: "Organizers report significant time savings" (implies variability, lower substantiation burden).
- Or: "One of our pilot customers measured a 60% reduction in inventory prep time" + disclose that results vary.

**Finding:** Before deploying the script, you must validate the 40-to-15 claim with documented customer evidence.

**Risk Level:** ⚠️ **MEDIUM** — Claim is supportable if customer/data exists; unsupportable if not.

### 12. **DROP COWBOY + TWILIO/BANDWIDTH A2P 10DLC COMPLIANCE**

**Summary:** If Drop Cowboy uses Twilio or Bandwidth, you must complete A2P 10DLC campaign registration.

**A2P 10DLC Workflow ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/compliance/a2p-10dlc)):**

1. **Brand Registration:**
   - Register FindA.Sale as the brand with the carriers.
   - Provide: company name, EIN, business type, address.
   - Twilio calls this "Customer Profile + Brand" in Trust Hub.

2. **Campaign Registration:**
   - Register this specific RVM campaign with detailed messaging content.
   - Provide: campaign name, description, message samples, opt-out language, monthly volume.
   - Carriers approve or reject based on compliance posture.

3. **Carrier Enforcement (as of Feb 2025):**
   - [Carriers block unregistered 10DLC SMS outright](https://getterms.io/blog/what-is-a2p-10dlc/).
   - If Drop Cowboy's SMS number is not registered, your SMS replies will silently fail (recipient won't receive the video link).

**How to Verify:**
- Ask Drop Cowboy: "Is our account A2P 10DLC registered with Twilio/Bandwidth/[provider]?"
- If using Twilio directly, log into Trust Hub and check Brand + Campaign status.
- If Drop Cowboy manages it, request proof of registration.

**Timeline Risk:**
- Registration can take 1–5 business days.
- Rejections are common for campaigns with weak opt-out language or unclear messaging intent.
- **Phase 1 timeline:** Begin A2P registration at least 2 weeks before planned RVM send date.

**Finding:** A2P 10DLC is a mandatory compliance step, not optional. Must be verified with Drop Cowboy + SMS provider before Phase 1.

**Risk Level:** 🔴 **BLOCKER** (if SMS is critical to campaign) — No A2P 10DLC registration = SMS delivery fails entirely.

## TOP 5 HIGHEST-RISK ITEMS

1. **🔴 Script Non-Compliance with State Law (BLOCKER)**
   - **Issue:** Version C does not include organization name + phone number at message beginning, violating federal TCPA and Michigan SB 351.
   - **Fix:** Rewrite first 10 seconds to: "[Caller name], FindA.Sale, [phone number]. We help estate sale organizers reduce inventory prep time…"
   - **Effort:** Low (script rewrite only).
   - **Timeline:** 1–2 hours.

2. **🔴 Missing Opt-Out Instructions in Audio (BLOCKER)**
   - **Issue:** Script does not include STOP text or voice callback option, violating FCC April 2025 opt-out rule.
   - **Fix:** Add to RVM end: "To stop these messages, text STOP to [number] or call [callback number]."
   - **Effort:** Low.
   - **Timeline:** 1 hour.

3. **🔴 A2P 10DLC Registration Not Verified (BLOCKER if SMS is campaign-critical)**
   - **Issue:** SMS reply leg (TEXT VIDEO) will fail entirely if A2P 10DLC campaign is not registered with carriers.
   - **Fix:** Confirm with Drop Cowboy + SMS provider that brand + campaign are registered. Request proof.
   - **Effort:** Medium (vendor coordination; may require campaign resubmission if rejected).
   - **Timeline:** 2–5 business days; plan 2 weeks before launch.

4. **⚠️ Persona Misrepresentation Risk (MEDIUM-HIGH)**
   - **Issue:** "I spent three years doing it" is a fictional persona claim that may violate FTC deceptive-practice standards.
   - **Fix:** Revise to: "We've worked with hundreds of operators who spent years manually tagging inventory. Here's what changed for one of them…" (if you have real customer).
   - **Effort:** Medium (script revision + customer consent if real testimonial used).
   - **Timeline:** 2–3 hours + customer approval.

5. **⚠️ Substantiation Gap for Quantified Claims (MEDIUM)**
   - **Issue:** "Cut prep time from 40 hours to under 15" requires documented customer proof; if not available, cannot be claimed.
   - **Fix:** Validate claim with real customer data + consent. If not available, soften to: "Operators report significant prep-time savings" (lower substantiation burden).
   - **Effort:** Medium (requires customer data audit).
   - **Timeline:** 3–5 hours + customer validation.
