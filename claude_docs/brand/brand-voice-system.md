## Overview

This document defines FindA.Sale's voice, tone, and messaging across all customer touchpoints. It extends the foundational brand-voice-guide with:

- **Explicit banned and approved language**
- **Tone shifts by context** (UI, email, social, outreach, errors)
- **10+ real before/after rewrites** from actual product copy
- **Channel-specific execution playbooks**
- **One-page quick reference card for writers**

**North Star:** "Find All The Sales" — a warm, practical platform celebrating all forms of community resale.

## Part 1: Voice Pillars

### Pillar 1: Helpful (Problem-Solver)

**What it means:**
We solve real, tangible problems. For organizers: reaching buyers, managing inventory, running sales with less manual work. For shoppers: discovering treasures, exploring their neighborhood, finding deals they actually want.

**How it shows up:**
- Acknowledge the specific pain point first ("Spreading the word about your sale takes time")
- Offer the concrete solution ("We reach serious local buyers automatically")
- Show confidence in the fix ("So you can focus on running the sale itself")

**What it's NOT:**
- Vague ("We make sales easier") — specify what problem you're solving
- Overly technical ("Leverage our proprietary recommendation algorithm") — jargon is friction
- Dismissive ("Just list your items") — doesn't acknowledge complexity

**Examples:**
- ✅ "List your items once. We handle reaching qualified buyers in your area."
- ❌ "Optimize your sale through intelligent buyer matching."
- ✅ "Searching for vintage furniture? Set an alert and we'll notify you when a match posts."
- ❌ "Our ML-powered discovery engine personalizes your browsing experience."

---

### Pillar 2: Honest (Transparent, No Hype)

**What it means:**
We tell you exactly how we work. No false promises, no dark patterns, no overstating what we do. Fee structure is clear. Limitations are acknowledged. We earn trust through candor.

**How it shows up:**
- State fees upfront and plainly
- Acknowledge what we don't do or can't promise
- Use precise language (not "maximize earnings" — "keep 90% of what you sell")
- Show we understand the real-world context (estate sales involve grief, garage sales are family events)

**What it's NOT:**
- Corporate-speak ("We're committed to creating synergies")
- Hype language ("game-changer," "disrupting," "revolutionary")
- Fake social proof or testimonials
- Euphemisms ("modest fee" instead of "10%")

**Examples:**
- ✅ "We charge 10%. You keep 90% of every sale."
- ❌ "We offer unbeatable commission rates that maximize your profitability."
- ✅ "We're new, so fewer shoppers than eBay. But everyone here is serious about buying."
- ❌ "Join millions of buyers on our cutting-edge marketplace."

---

### Pillar 3: Inclusive (All Sale Types Welcome)

**What it means:**
Estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets, consignment — all are celebrated equally. No one sale type is the default. Language and examples reflect the full breadth. No gatekeeping.

**How it shows up:**
- Naturally reference 2–3 sale types per copy block (not "estate sales, oh and also yard sales")
- Show equally professional regard for large and small events
- Acknowledge different emotional contexts (estate sales → grief; garage sales → family memories; auctions → bidding excitement)
- Use examples that span tiers and sale types

**What it's NOT:**
- Estate-sale-centric ("For estate liquidators and similar events")
- Dismissive of casual sales ("From serious auctions to casual garage sales")
- Treating one type as premium and others as basic
- Forgetting that flea markets, consignment, and rummage sales exist

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Estate sales, garage sales, auctions, flea markets — all kinds of secondary resale."
- ❌ "Professional estate sales, and also for garage sale hosts looking to expand."
- ✅ "Whether you're running an estate company, hosting a neighborhood garage sale, or auctioning collectibles, we get the work involved."
- ❌ "Our platform serves estate liquidators and other types of sales."

---

### Pillar 4: Practical (Focus on What Works)

**What it means:**
We focus on real, measurable outcomes. Not aspirational messaging. Not feel-good positioning. What actually happens when organizers list, what shoppers actually do, what works in the market.

**How it shows up:**
- Lead with outcomes, not features ("reach serious buyers" not "advanced algorithm")
- Use concrete numbers when available ("Your items stay visible for 30 days" vs "for a long time")
- Avoid future-tense promises ("will be able to" — vague timing)
- Speak in terms of tasks, not abstract concepts

**What it's NOT:**
- Theoretical ("This enables you to optimize your market position")
- Marketing hyperbole ("Unlock unlimited potential")
- Futuristic jargon ("Intelligent platform," "smart matching")
- Promises without specifics ("We help you sell more")

**Examples:**
- ✅ "List your items once. Our system surfaces them to 5,000+ active shoppers in your area."
- ❌ "Leverage our intelligent distribution network for optimal market penetration."
- ✅ "Sold out? Add your next batch without re-entering basic details."
- ❌ "Streamlined workflows empower rapid inventory cycling."

---

### Pillar 5: Curious (Love of Discovery)

**What it means:**
For shoppers, we celebrate the hunt. The joy of finding something unexpected. For organizers, we respect their expertise and the curation they've done (they know their items; we help them find buyers).

**How it shows up:**
- For shoppers: Use language of discovery ("Find," "Explore," "Discover," "Hunt")
- For organizers: Position them as the expert ("You know your items, we reach the buyers")
- Acknowledge the fun, the community, the social aspect
- Celebrate the variety and uniqueness of secondhand treasures

**What it's NOT:**
- Clinical or transactional ("Browse inventory")
- Dismissive of secondhand ("Used goods," "getting rid of stuff")
- Focusing only on price ("the cheapest deals")
- Solo experience (ignoring community aspect)

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Every weekend is an adventure. Browse sales near you, find what you didn't know you were looking for."
- ❌ "Compare prices on used merchandise across multiple liquidators."
- ✅ "Follow your favorite organizers and get notified when they post new treasures."
- ❌ "Subscribe to merchant notifications for faster access to inventory."

## Part 2: Banned Words & Phrases

These terms are **explicitly forbidden** across all customer-facing copy (UI, email, marketing, error messages, onboarding). Violations get flagged in brand review.

### Banned Permanently (Per Decisions Log)

| Phrase | Why Banned | Use Instead |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| "AI" (in product names or UI) | D-006: Overstates our role, confuses shoppers. Too salesy in beta phase | "Smart," "Auto," "Suggested," or just don't mention it |
| "Artificial Intelligence" (same) | Same reason | Same replacements |
| "estate sale" (as sole sale type reference) | D-001: Exclusionary. Ignores the full breadth of resale | "Estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets" |
| "disrupting" / "disruption" | Overblown, corporate cliché | Describe what you actually do: "help," "simplify," "connect" |
| "game-changer" | Silicon Valley hype, undermines honesty pillar | Describe the specific benefit |
| "passionate about" + vague value | Generic, non-specific | Name the actual benefit: "We believe second-hand treasures deserve to be found" |
| "access to [X]" (when no barriers exist) | False scarcity | Just say "discover" or "browse" |
| Founder voice ("I," "we founded," personal names in copy) | D-S268: Institutional brand, not personality brand | "The FindA.Sale Team," "We," "Our platform" |
| "optimize," "leverage," "synergy" | Jargon, not practical language | Use action verbs instead |

---

### Context-Specific Bans

**In Error Messages:**
- ❌ "An error occurred" (meaningless)
- ✅ "We couldn't save your item. Please try again or contact support if it keeps happening."

**In Empty States:**
- ❌ "No results found"
- ✅ "No sales posted yet in [city]. Check back soon or follow nearby cities."

**In Onboarding:**
- ❌ "Set up your account to unlock features"
- ✅ "Create your account to list your sale"

**In Emails (Marketing/Outreach):**
- ❌ "exclusive offers," "limited time," "act now"
- ✅ "Your sale is live," "3 organizing tips," "Serious buyers are already browsing"

## Part 3: Approved Phrases & Language Patterns

These phrases align with voice pillars and should appear regularly in copy.

### Approved Action Verbs

Use these in CTAs, headlines, and instructions:
- "Find" (primary for shoppers)
- "Discover" (primary for shoppers)
- "Explore" (secondary for shoppers)
- "List" (primary for organizers — neutral, clear)
- "Connect" (implies person-to-person, not algorithmic)
- "Reach" (practical outcome for organizers)
- "Manage" (capability for organizers)
- "Browse" (for discovery, less intense than "hunt")

---

### Approved Benefit Language

Use these when describing outcomes:

**For Organizers:**
- "Reach [number] serious local buyers"
- "Keep 90% of every sale"
- "List once, reach thousands nearby"
- "Focus on [actual task], we handle [distribution/logistics/marketing]"
- "[Specific outcome] without [manual work they hate]"

**For Shoppers:**
- "Find secondhand treasures happening this weekend"
- "Discover what you didn't know you were looking for"
- "Great deals on [category] near you"
- "Follow your favorite organizers and get notified when they post"

---

### Approved Modifiers

These reinforce inclusivity and honesty:

- "all kinds of" (estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets)
- "across your region" or "near you" (sets realistic expectations)
- "serious buyers" (implies we filter, not just "any buyer")
- "straightforward," "simple," "no surprises"
- "whether you're" (introducing examples of who we serve)

---

### Approved Secondary Language

For descriptions, explanations, deeper copy:

- "We get the work involved" (shows empathy, honesty)
- "You know your items, we find the buyers" (respect organizer expertise)
- "From rare collectibles to everyday bargains" (span of treasure types)
- "No contracts, no obligations" (removing friction, honesty)
- "Community of treasure hunters" (inclusive, social)

## Part 4: Before/After Rewrites

Real examples from FindA.Sale product, marketing, and emails. Each shows the voice pillar being corrected.

### Example 1: Homepage Hero (Pillar 1: Helpful)

**Before:**
> "The marketplace designed for community resale events. Powered by intelligent buyer matching and AI-driven discovery."

**Issues:** Jargon ("intelligent buyer matching," "AI-driven"), unclear benefit, mentions features not outcomes

**After:**
> "Find All The Sales. Estate sales, garage sales, auctions, flea markets — discover secondhand treasures happening near you right now."

**What improved:** Specific benefit (find treasures), all sale types mentioned, practical outcome (near you, right now)

---

### Example 2: Organizer CTA (Pillar 2: Honest)

**Before:**
> "Join FindA.Sale and unlock your earnings potential with our industry-leading commission structure."

**Issues:** "Unlock" implies gatekeeping, "industry-leading" is unverified hype, vague

**After:**
> "List your sale in minutes. Reach serious local buyers. Keep 90% of what you sell."

**What improved:** Three concrete outcomes in one sentence, fee is transparent (90% = 10% implied), no fluff

---

### Example 3: Email Subject Line (Pillar 3: Inclusive)

**Before:**
> "Your estate sale went viral — 500+ views from serious collectors"

**Issues:** "Viral" is hype, assumes event type (what if it's a garage sale?), misleading engagement

**After:**
> "Your sale has 500+ views. Serious buyers are browsing right now."

**What improved:** Honest about engagement (not "viral"), no assumptions about event type, practical implication (action is browsing)

---

### Example 4: Onboarding Message (Pillar 4: Practical)

**Before:**
> "Welcome to FindA.Sale! We're thrilled to help you optimize your selling experience with cutting-edge technology and AI-powered tools."

**Issues:** Marketing fluff, no specific benefit, jargon ("cutting-edge," "optimize")

**After:**
> "Welcome! Here's how to list your first sale and reach local buyers in your area."

**What improved:** Task-focused (list first sale), realistic scope (local buyers), no hype

---

### Example 5: Shopper Empty State (Pillar 5: Curious)

**Before:**
> "No inventory available for your search criteria. Refine filters or try another location."

**Issues:** Clinical, closes the door (negative), doesn't invite exploration

**After:**
> "No sales posted yet in [City] for [Category]. Want to keep looking? Browse [Nearby City] or follow this category to get notified when new items post nearby."

**What improved:** Empathetic tone, offers alternatives, invites discovery (follow → notification), opens doors

---

### Example 6: Organizer Tier Messaging (Pillar 2: Honest)

**Before:**
> "Upgrade to PRO and unlock premium features for maximum visibility."

**Issues:** "Unlock," "premium," "maximum" are all hype. What does PRO actually give you?

**After:**
> "PRO ($29/month): List up to 500 items per sale, add 10 photos per item, reach up to 50,000 regional shoppers."

**What improved:** Specific limits, concrete reach, no "premium" framing (just features)

---

### Example 7: Error Message (Pillar 1: Helpful)

**Before:**
> "An error occurred. Please try again later or contact support."

**Issues:** Vague, doesn't tell user what went wrong, no next steps

**After:**
> "We couldn't save your price. Make sure it's a valid number (like $25 or $19.99) and try again. Still stuck? Let us know at support@finda.sale."

**What improved:** Specific error (price format), clear fix, alternative (email support)

---

### Example 8: Social Post (Pillar 5: Curious + Inclusive)

**Before:**
> "Estate sale organizers, are you struggling to reach qualified buyers? Try our AI-powered platform and increase your profits TODAY."

**Issues:** Single sale type, fake urgency (caps), jargon ("AI-powered"), assumes profit is only motivation

**After:**
> "Running an auction? Hosting a garage sale? Auctioneers, estate companies, and individual organizers — they all use FindA.Sale to reach serious buyers. What sale are you running?"

**What improved:** Inclusive of all types, specific to each, conversational tone, invites them to see themselves in it

---

### Example 9: Shopper Referral Copy (Pillar 5: Curious)

**Before:**
> "Unlock exclusive rewards by inviting friends to the platform."

**Issues:** "Unlock" implies gatekeeping, "exclusive" is hype (they're not actually exclusive)

**After:**
> "Share your favorite sales with friends. When they sign up and make a purchase, you both get 500 XP toward a free Hunt Pass."

**What improved:** Concrete outcome (free Hunt Pass), specific benefit per action (sign up + purchase), no false exclusivity

---

### Example 10: Admin/Organizer Help Text (Pillar 1: Helpful)

**Before:**
> "Configure your seller preferences to optimize the buyer experience."

**Issues:** Jargon ("optimize"), abstract ("buyer experience"), not task-focused

**After:**
> "Set your hours and location so shoppers know when and where to pick up. This goes on your public sale listing."

**What improved:** Specific task (set hours/location), clear outcome (appears on listing), practical context (pickup)

## Part 5: Tone by Context

Voice is consistent. Tone shifts based on context.

### UI Microcopy (Buttons, Labels, Empty States, Tooltips)

**Characteristics:**
- Extremely concise (2–5 words ideal)
- Action-oriented
- Practical, not explanatory
- Conversational contractions OK (don't, can't, won't)

**Tone markers:**
- Buttons: "[Action] [Object]" — "List Sale," "Add Items," "Save as Draft"
- Empty states: Acknowledge the state, offer next step
- Tooltips: Answer "why would I do this?" in one sentence
- Errors: State the problem + the fix

**Examples:**
- ✅ Button: "Add Photos" (not "Upload Images to This Sale")
- ✅ Empty state: "No items yet. Start adding items to your sale." (not "Your inventory is empty.")
- ✅ Tooltip: "Shoppers will see this price when browsing your sale."
- ✅ Error: "Photo must be under 10MB. You uploaded 15MB. Try a smaller file or compress first."

---

### Email (Transactional + Marketing)

**Characteristics:**
- Warmer and more personal than UI
- Clear hierarchy: subject line hooks → body explains → CTA is obvious
- Sign-off from "The FindA.Sale Team" (not person)
- Concrete numbers and actions

**Tone markers:**
- Subject lines: Curiosity or outcome ("3 sales started near you" vs "New sales in Grand Rapids")
- Opening: Acknowledge their action or milestone ("Your sale went live!")
- Body: Concrete info, minimal jargon, one idea per paragraph
- CTA: Specific and urgent (time-bound if real: "Sale ends Saturday" not "Sale ending soon")
- Signature: "The FindA.Sale Team" (institutional)

**Examples:**

*Organizer transactional:*
> Subject: Your sale is live — serious buyers are browsing right now
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Your [Sale Type] is live and visible to 12,000+ shoppers in your area. Here's what's happening:
>
> - 47 people have already viewed your listing
> - 8 items have been added to wishlists
> - Your sale will stay visible for 30 days
>
> Next step: Watch for shopper questions in your messages, and update your sale if you add or sell items.
> 
> Questions? Reply to this email or visit our help center.
> 
> The FindA.Sale Team

*Shopper discovery:*
> Subject: 5 sales starting Saturday near you (including that vintage dresser)
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> New sales just posted in your area:
>
> - Estate Sale, Downtown (80+ items) — Starts Sat 8am
> - Garage Sale, Midtown (Furniture & Collectibles) — Sat 9am
> - Outdoor Auction, North End — Sun 10am
>
> And we found a mid-century dresser matching your style in one of these sales. Browse now before the best items sell.
>
> The FindA.Sale Team

---

### Social Media (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn)

**Characteristics:**
- Platform-native tone (more casual on TikTok, more professional on LinkedIn)
- Shorter form
- Use of emojis and line breaks
- Community-oriented (tag examples, celebrate user content)
- Call for interaction (questions, polls, asks)

**Tone markers:**
- Instagram: Celebratory, visual-first, hashtag discovery
- Facebook: Community and local focus, longer captions OK
- TikTok: Casual, behind-the-scenes, trends OK if authentic
- LinkedIn: Professional but warm, thought leadership on resale

**Examples:**

*Instagram (community):*
> 🔍 "Found a vintage leather chair hiding in a garage sale. It's the hunt that makes it exciting. 
>
> What's the best treasure you've ever found? Drop it below 👇"

*Facebook (local focus):*
> "Running a sale this weekend in Grand Rapids? 
>
> List it free on FindA.Sale and reach 5,000+ serious buyers in your area. We handle the online reach — you focus on the sale itself.
>
> Fair warning: your best items might sell on Friday night. 😄"

*LinkedIn (thought leadership):*
> "Spoiler: The resale market is thriving. Organizers of ALL types — estate companies, garage sale hosts, auctioneers — are seeing 30%+ growth in buyer interest.
>
> Why? Because secondhand treasures are local, sustainable, and full of personality.
>
> If you run secondary sales, now is the time to build your platform."

---

### Cold Outreach Email (B2B Organizer Acquisition)

**Characteristics:**
- Institutional sender ("The FindA.Sale Team" not "Hi from Patrick")
- Solution-focused, not pitch-focused
- Respects their existing expertise
- Curiosity-driven (asks a question that matters)
- No false urgency or pressure

**Tone markers:**
- Opening: Acknowledge their business, show respect
- Hook: Ask a genuine question (not "would you like to grow?" but something specific to their type)
- Body: Show how we help (not feature list, but outcomes their peers see)
- CTA: Soft and low-pressure ("Let's chat" or "Curious?" not "Call now")

**Example (for estate sale companies):**

> **Subject:** Where do your buyers discover your sales today?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I noticed you run [Company Name] estate sales across [Region]. Curious question: where do most of your serious buyers find out about your sales?
>
> We talk to organizers like you, and the answer is usually: "Multiple sites, lots of manual posting, and word-of-mouth." It works, but it's time-intensive.
>
> We built FindA.Sale specifically for estate companies, garage sale hosts, and auctioneers. List once, and we surface your sales to serious local buyers automatically. Most organizers tell us they spend less time on marketing and sell 20%+ faster.
>
> No pressure, but if you're curious how other [Region] organizers are using the platform, I'd be happy to show you.
>
> Cheers,  
> The FindA.Sale Team  
> hello@finda.sale

### Support/Help (Troubleshooting, FAQs, Knowledge Base)

**Characteristics:**
- Acknowledge the problem first (shows empathy)
- Explain the "why" (builds trust)
- Give clear steps (task-focused)
- Offer escalation gracefully (don't leave them stuck)

**Tone markers:**
- Start with the problem statement, not the solution
- Use "here's why" to explain system behavior
- Numbered steps for complex tasks
- "Still stuck?" → offer support channel

**Example:**

> **My items aren't showing up on search. Why?**
>
> If your items are in Draft or Pending Review status, they won't appear in shopper search yet. Here's why: We give you time to add photos and details before they go live.
>
> To publish your items and make them searchable:
> 1. Go to Manage Items
> 2. Click the item you want to publish
> 3. Click "Publish" — it'll take 2–3 minutes to show up in search
>
> **Still seeing drafts?** Items stay in draft if you save them without hitting publish. Check your "Draft Items" section and publish from there.
>
> Still stuck? Email support@finda.sale with a screenshot and we'll help.

## Part 6: Quick Reference Card for Writers

Print this. Post it. Use it before every piece of copy.

### Brand Voice Checklist

Before publishing ANY copy, ask:

- [ ] **Helpful?** Does it solve a real problem or answer a real question?
- [ ] **Honest?** No hype, no fluff? Are fees/limitations transparent?
- [ ] **Inclusive?** All sale types represented fairly (or is single-type OK for this context)?
- [ ] **Practical?** Specific outcomes, not abstract concepts?
- [ ] **Curious?** For shoppers, does it celebrate the hunt? For organizers, respect their expertise?

### Banned Word Check

Does the copy contain ANY of these?
- [ ] "AI" or "Artificial Intelligence" — Replace with "Smart," "Auto," or don't mention
- [ ] "estate sale" (as sole example) — Add other sale types
- [ ] "disrupting," "game-changer," "leverage," "optimize" — Rewrite in plain language
- [ ] Founder voice ("I," person's name) — Change to "We" or "The FindA.Sale Team"
- [ ] "exclusive," "unlock," "limited time" — Rewrite without scarcity tactics

### Context-Specific Tone Check

| Context | Tone Check |
|---------|-----------|
| **UI (button/label/empty state)** | 2–5 words? Action-verb first? Practical (not explanatory)? |
| **Email (subject line)** | Does it hook with outcome or curiosity? No urgency unless time-sensitive? |
| **Email (body)** | Concrete numbers? One idea per paragraph? Soft CTA? Signed "The FindA.Sale Team"? |
| **Social** | Platform-native tone? Community-focused? Invitation to engage? |
| **Cold outreach** | Institutional sender? Respects their expertise? Curiosity-driven question first? |
| **Help/Support** | Acknowledges problem first? Explains "why"? Clear steps? Escalation path? |

## Part 7: Implementation Guidance

### For Product Managers & Designers
- Include a "Copy Tone Check" step in your feature spec. Assign it to a writer.
- Flag copy violations in design review. Don't ship with jargon, hype, or banned terms.
- Use this document as your acceptance criteria for copy.

### For Content Teams (Email, Social, Help)
- Review all copy monthly against this guide.
- Flag violations in a shared doc (not as blame — as continuous improvement).
- Update this guide when you discover new edge cases.

### For Developers
- Copy-paste error messages from the "error messages" section of this guide. Don't invent.
- When in doubt about placeholder text, reference approved examples.
- Flag vague requirements like "say something inspiring" — send to writer instead.

### For Patrick (Founder)
- Use "The FindA.Sale Team" as sender for all outreach, emails, social.
- Do not use personal names or "founder voice" in customer-facing copy.
- Review major campaigns against Part 4 (before/after) and Part 6 (checklist) before launch.
