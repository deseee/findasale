# Second-Hand Reselling Series — Plan, Outlines & Opus Prompts

**Premise:** A young woman documents her journey building a second-hand reselling business. The videos are about *her* — the hunt, the wins, the flops, the money — not about FindA.Sale. The product appears the way a real tool appears in a real workflow: she uses it to find sales to source from, and (later) to run her own. Featured, never pitched.

**Why this works:** A reseller naturally lives on *both* sides of the marketplace. She uses FindA.Sale as a **shopper** to discover estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets, and consignment to source inventory — and eventually as an **organizer** to run her own sell-offs. That dual role lets the product show up dozens of times across a season without a single ad read.

---

## Part 1 — What's working in video right now (2026)

**The format that's winning is "documented, not produced."** Build-in-public storytelling shortens the gap between discovery and trust. Audiences in 2026 reward honesty, vulnerability, and showing the real numbers over polish. This is good news for two people with little shooting experience — the aesthetic *is* low-effort. "Elevated casual" (looks intentional, not corporate) is the Reels standard; raw authenticity wins on TikTok.

**One shoot, many cuts.** The efficient model is to film one sourcing trip or one work session, then cut it into: one long-form YouTube piece (8–15 min) plus 4–8 verticals for TikTok / Shorts / Reels / Facebook video. You are not making 8 videos a week; you are making one and slicing it.

**Length targets by platform:**
- TikTok: 15–60s, optimize for *rewatch*
- Reels: 30–60s, optimize for *saves and shares* (a "save" is the secret weapon)
- Shorts: 60–90s, lean *educational / how-much-did-it-sell-for*
- YouTube long-form: 8–15 min, target 50%+ average view; the first 30 seconds decide everything

**The 3-second rule is non-negotiable.** Up to 50% of viewers leave in the first three seconds. Open on the result or the tension ("I paid $4 for this. It just sold for $90."), never on a slow intro or a logo.

**Posting cadence that's realistic:** TikTok 1–2/day, Reels 4–7/week, Shorts 3–5/week, YouTube 1/week. From one weekly shoot you can hit most of this by slicing.

**Proven reseller formats** (steal these): the haul reveal, the "what sold this week + the profit," sourcing ride-alongs, the flip transformation (clean/repair/restyle), mystery-box bundles, and the price-guessing hook ("guess what this sold for").

### Gear — the whole kit, kept cheap
Audio matters more than camera. Viewers forgive shaky video; bad audio kills watch time instantly. In priority order:
1. **A wireless lav mic** (~$50–70, e.g. a budget clip-on / Rode-class). Single biggest upgrade.
2. **The phone she already owns.** Don't buy a camera.
3. **A flexible GorillaPod-style tripod** for awkward angles and table-top flip shots.
4. **A window.** Face it, never stand in front of it. Free, beautiful light. One small LED panel (~$30) for night listing sessions.

That's it. Total under ~$150. Everything else is editing, which is free on CapCut.

## Part 2 — Two series outlines

### Outline A — "Flipping for a Living" (the underdog money story)
**Spine:** Can she replace a paycheck with reselling? Each episode tracks a real goal — "Week 3: can I clear $300 profit?" — with the running total shown on screen every episode. Money is the throughline; the product is the tool she sources with.

**Long-form arc (weekly, ~10 min each):**
1. *The $100 Challenge* — turn $100 of sourcing into as much profit as possible in one week. Establishes her, the stakes, the scoreboard.
2. *The Estate Sale Goldmine* — a full sourcing trip; she finds the sales the night before on FindA.Sale, maps her route, films the hunt.
3. *Everything I Bought Is Wrong* — the flop episode. Honest losses. Builds enormous trust.
4. *Flip It Till It Sells* — cleaning, repairing, restyling; the satisfying transformation format.
5. *My First $1,000 Month* — the payoff episode + what she learned.

**Short-form slices from each shoot:** the haul reveal, "guess the sale price," the single best find, the biggest loss, a 30-sec sourcing route tip, a listing-desk time-lapse.

**Best for:** fastest emotional pull, strongest retention (built-in scoreboard), most relatable. **Risk:** requires her to actually be transparent about money.

---

### Outline B — "Sourced" (the treasure-hunt lifestyle channel)
**Spine:** Less about profit, more about the *hunt* and the *finds*. Aesthetic, calming, "come thrifting with me" energy. The business grows quietly in the background; the foreground is the joy of discovery and the stories behind objects.

**Long-form arc (weekly, ~8–12 min):**
1. *5 Sales, One Saturday* — a "with me" sourcing marathon across estate / yard / flea in one day.
2. *The Thing Nobody Wanted* — a deep dive on one weird/beautiful object: its history, its restoration, who finally bought it.
3. *Dead Malls & Flea Markets* — destination/road-trip episode; location as character.
4. *Setting Up My Own Sale* — she crosses over to the organizer side and runs her first yard/estate-style sell-off (FindA.Sale as the tool to list and publish it).
5. *A Season of Finds* — montage payoff, community Q&A.

**Short-form slices:** ASMR cleaning clips, "find of the day," object-history micro-stories, route/map reveals, the calm listing time-lapse.

**Best for:** brand safety, evergreen/searchable content, broad appeal, very low pressure to perform financially. **Risk:** slower to build a "must-watch" hook than the money story.

**My recommendation:** Lead with **Outline A** for the YouTube spine (the scoreboard drives retention and subscribes), and borrow **Outline B's** calmer object-story and ASMR formats for short-form variety. Run A as the show, B as the texture.

## Part 3 — Opus prompts (ready to paste)

Two prompts below. The first generates a full season production plan; the second is a repeatable per-episode engine you'll reuse every week. Fill the brackets before pasting.

### Prompt 1 — Season architect (run once)

```
You are a content strategist and short-form video producer planning a documentary-style
YouTube + short-form series. Do NOT make it an ad.

THE SHOW
A young woman (call her [NAME / or "the host"]) documents her journey building a
second-hand reselling business from scratch. The videos are about HER journey — the
sourcing hunts, the wins, the flops, and the real money — not about any product.
A marketplace app called FindA.Sale appears naturally inside her real workflow: she
uses it as a shopper to discover estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets, and
consignment sales to source inventory, and later as an organizer to run her own sale.
The app is featured the way a real tool appears in a real workflow — shown in use,
never pitched, never explained like a commercial. Never use the word "AI" in any
on-screen text or script. No founder is named; this is the host's personal story.

CONSTRAINTS
- Two-person crew (host + one helper), minimal video experience.
- Gear: a phone, one clip-on wireless mic, a small tripod, window light, free editing (CapCut).
- One filming session per week must yield: 1 long-form YouTube (8–15 min) + 6–8 vertical
  clips for TikTok, Shorts, Reels, and Facebook video.
- 2026 best practices: 3-second hook or viewers leave; TikTok rewards rewatch, Reels
  rewards saves/shares, Shorts leans educational, YouTube long-form needs 50%+ retention.

DELIVERABLES
1. A series bible: title options (5), one-line premise, the host's character/voice, the
   season-long "spine" or goal that creates a scoreboard for retention, and the tone.
2. A 10-episode season plan. For each episode: working title, the hook (first 3 seconds,
   written verbatim), the story arc, what's filmed, where FindA.Sale naturally appears,
   and the 6–8 short-form clips that can be sliced from that one shoot.
3. A weekly production cadence and a realistic posting calendar across YouTube, TikTok,
   Shorts, Reels, and Facebook video.
4. A "one shoot, many cuts" workflow: exactly how to film one sourcing trip so it yields
   all the cuts above with the least possible effort and editing.
5. The 10 strongest short-form hook lines for this niche, written verbatim, ready to shoot.

Be concrete and production-ready. Assume the crew has never done this before.
```

### Prompt 2 — Weekly episode engine (reuse every week)

```
You are my weekly video producer for a documentary-style reselling series. The show
follows a young woman building a second-hand reselling business; videos are about her
journey, not a product. A marketplace app, FindA.Sale, appears naturally when she sources
sales (estate, yard, auction, flea, consignment) or runs her own. Never say "AI." Never

THIS WEEK'S RAW MATERIAL:
[Paste what actually happened this week: where she sourced, what she bought, what it cost,
what sold, for how much, any flops, any funny/emotional moments, any photos or clips you have.]

GIVE ME:
1. One long-form YouTube episode (8–15 min): a verbatim first-30-second hook, a beat-by-beat
   outline, the on-screen scoreboard/numbers to show, and where FindA.Sale appears in frame.
2. 6–8 vertical clips sliced from the same footage. For each: platform (TikTok/Shorts/Reels/
   FB), target length, the verbatim 3-second hook, the payoff, and the on-screen caption.
3. Titles + thumbnail text for the long-form (3 options each).
4. Captions/descriptions per platform with hashtags, optimized for the 2026 algorithm
   (TikTok=rewatch, Reels=saves/shares, Shorts=educational, YouTube=retention).
5. A 30-second shot list telling the crew exactly what to film next week so editing is easy.

Keep hooks honest and human, not hypey. Lead with the result or the tension, never a slow intro.
```

## Suggested first move
Pick the host and shoot **one** pilot sourcing trip this week using Prompt 1's output as the season map and Prompt 2 to cut the pilot. Post the YouTube long-form once, then drip the 6–8 verticals across the week. Judge from real retention data after two weeks, then commit to Outline A or B.
