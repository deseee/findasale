# Submit Items for Community Appraisals

When you're unsure about an item's value—a piece of jewelry, a collectible, a vintage piece—you can submit it for a community appraisal. Experienced buyers and resellers on FindA.Sale can review the photos and details and suggest a fair market price based on their expertise.

This is useful when an item feels valuable but you're not confident pricing it. Get input before listing, then decide your asking price.

For full details on how the appraisal process works, scoring systems, and how to respond to feedback, see Community Appraisals in your account settings.

## When to use appraisals

**Submit for appraisal if:**
- The item is jewelry, watches, or precious metals (hard to price without expertise)
- It's a collectible (vintage toys, cards, memorabilia)
- It's antique furniture or art (provenance and rarity matter)
- You're uncertain whether it's actually valuable or just looks expensive
- It's a brand-name designer item and you want to confirm authenticity

**Don't submit if:**
- It's obviously common (generic kitchenware, recent mass-produced furniture)
- You already priced it and shoppers are buying it fine
- You don't need feedback (you're confident in your price)

## How to submit

**1. Find the item in your inventory**
Go to your sale. Tap on the item you want appraised.

**2. Tap "Request Appraisal"**
A form appears asking:
- Why you want an appraisal (uncertain value, authentication, pricing advice)
- Any details about the item (maker, era, materials, condition)
- Upload 3–5 clear photos (close-ups of details, hallmarks, wear)

**3. Submit**
Click "Submit for Appraisal." Your item goes into the community appraisal queue.

**4. Wait for feedback**
Community members (other organizers, resellers, experts) review your item over the next few days and submit suggested prices and comments. You'll see responses come in over 48–72 hours.

**5. Read the feedback**
Each appraiser suggests a price range and explains their reasoning. You might get:
- "This is 1970s Pyrex, great condition, $25–35"
- "The hallmark says [maker], weight is solid silver, conservative estimate $400–500"
- "Condition is worn, price accordingly as is $10–15"

**6. Decide your price**
Based on the feedback, you set your listing price. You don't have to follow the appraisal—it's advice, not law. If appraisers say $25–35 but you want to ask $40, you can. (You might not sell it, but it's your choice.)

## Why appraisals work

Experienced buyers have seen thousands of items. They know which eras, makers, and conditions command higher prices. Their input helps you:
- Avoid underpricing something valuable
- Avoid overpricing and scaring away buyers
- Understand what makes an item special (provenance, rarity, condition)

## Common questions

**Q: Is an appraisal binding?**
A: No. It's advice. You can price however you want.

**Q: Can I appraise an item that's already listed?**
A: Yes, but once it's getting traffic and selling fine, an appraisal doesn't help. Best to appraise before listing.

**Q: Who are the appraisers?**
A: Other FindA.Sale users (organizers, resellers, collectors). They're vetted community members, not professional appraisers.

**Q: Do I pay for appraisals?**
A: No. Community appraisals are free.

**Q: What if I get wildly different suggested prices?**
A: It happens. If appraisers disagree (one says $50, another says $200), it usually means the item's condition or authenticity is unclear. Ask clarifying questions in the comments and use your best judgment.

**Q: Can I appeal an appraisal I disagree with?**
A: You can respond with additional information (e.g., a maker's mark, documentation of authenticity, condition details) and ask for a revised appraisal.

**Q: How long does an appraisal take?**
A: 24–72 hours typically. You can view responses as they come in.
