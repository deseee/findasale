# Color Rules: Use Tag Colors for In-Person Sorting

At a yard sale, estate sale, or flea market, your staff needs to move fast. Color-coded tags let them spot items by price tier, consignor, or category without reading labels. Color rules automate this — assign a color to a tag, and every item with that tag automatically displays the color in your dashboard and on print-ready price tags.

## How it works

**Set up a color rule.**

1. Go to **Settings** → **Color Rules**.
2. Create a new rule. Choose a tag (e.g., "Premium") and assign it a color (e.g., red).
3. Repeat for other tags. You might have:
   - Blue = Regular inventory ($5–$25)
   - Green = Discount/Clearance ($1–$5)
   - Red = Premium ($50+)
   - Yellow = Consignment (track by vendor)
   - Orange = Fragile (handling note)

**Apply tags in the inventory.**

Tags are part of your item data. When you create or edit an item, add relevant tags. The color rule applies automatically — if the item has the "Premium" tag and your rule says Premium = red, the item shows red in your inventory view and on its price tag.

**Use colors at your sale.**

Print your inventory sheet (with colors) or display it on a tablet as your staff prices and moves items. A staff member looking for items under $10 scans for green tags. Premium section? Look for red. This speeds up physical setup and prevents mis-pricing.

## Why color rules matter

Without color rules, your staff reads every single item label to find items by price or category. With them, visual scanning does 80% of the work. At a large estate sale with 500+ items, that saves hours.

Color rules also reduce checkout errors. If a customer picks up a blue item and the register shows it should be green, you know something's wrong — the item got moved or mis-tagged during setup.

## Step-by-step setup

**Step 1: Identify your categories.**
What matters most at your sale? Price tiers, consignor groups, condition levels, or product categories? Start with 3–5 categories max. Too many colors dilute the benefit.

**Step 2: Create tags.**
Go to **Settings** → **Manage Tags**. Create one tag per category (e.g., "Tier-Budget", "Tier-Standard", "Tier-Premium", "Consignment-Jones", "Fragile").

**Step 3: Create color rules.**
In **Settings** → **Color Rules**, map each tag to a color. Test your choices on a few items to make sure the colors are visually distinct in print and on screen.

**Step 4: Tag your inventory.**
As you enter items or at a batch-tagging session, apply the relevant tags. Use the bulk-tag feature if you have multiple items in the same category.

**Step 5: Print or display.**
Generate your inventory sheet with colors included. Print it or pull it up on a tablet at the sale. Your staff uses it as their setup guide.

## Common questions

**Q: Can I change a color rule after my sale starts?**
A: Yes. The color updates everywhere instantly — on your dashboard, printed sheets, and any displays. But if you've already printed labels, they won't update. Only change rules before printing or be ready to reprint.

**Q: Do shoppers see the color tags?**
A: No. Colors are for your staff. Shoppers see regular price tags or QR codes depending on your setup.

**Q: What if an item has multiple tags?**
A: The item displays the first tag's color. Choose your tag order carefully — place the most important category tag first.

**Q: Can I use color rules for condition tracking?**
A: Yes. Create tags like "As-Is", "Good", "Like-New" and color-code them. This helps staff quickly identify items that need closer inspection or carry disclosures.

**Q: Do color rules work on mobile?**
A: Yes. If you use the organizer app to manage inventory during setup, colors display the same way as on the web.

**Q: Can I reuse color rules for multiple sales?**
A: Yes. Once you set up a rule, save it as a template. Next sale, load the template and adjust tags as needed. This saves setup time.
