# Categories, Tags, and Why They Affect Searchability

Your item's category and tags are how shoppers find it. When someone searches "dining chairs" or filters by "wood furniture," they see your listings. Get these right and shoppers find your best items.

## How categories work

Every item belongs to one category: Furniture, Kitchen, Décor, Electronics, Clothing, Books, Toys, Sports, Collectibles, etc.

The app auto-assigns a category based on the photo. In the review queue, you can change it to be more specific.

**Auto-assigned category:** Furniture  
**Better category:** Dining Room Furniture

**Auto-assigned category:** Home Décor  
**Better category:** Wall Art

Specific categories help shoppers filter results. A shopper looking for "Furniture" sees 2,000 listings. A shopper filtering "Dining Room Furniture" sees 200. Your item is more likely to be noticed.

## How tags work

Tags are attributes that describe the item: material (wood, metal, ceramic), color (blue, red, oak), style (vintage, modern, rustic), size, era, brand, etc.

The app auto-detects tags from the photo. In the review queue, you can add or edit tags.

**Example tags:**
- Material: wood, metal, ceramic, glass, fabric
- Color: blue, walnut, chrome, painted
- Style: vintage, mid-century, cottage, industrial, modern
- Era: 1950s, Victorian, antique
- Brand: Ethan Allen, IKEA, Stickley
- Condition-relevant: handmade, signed, one-of-a-kind

## Why these matter

Shoppers search and filter by category and tags. If someone searches "wood dining chair" and your chair is tagged "wood" and "dining," it appears. If it's tagged "fabric" and "office," it doesn't.

More specific tags = higher relevance = more shoppers see your listing = faster sales.

## How to edit categories and tags

In the review queue, tap the item. Tap "Category" or "Tags." Add, remove, or edit as needed.

**Add relevant tags:**
- If you have a 1950s lamp, add the era tag "1950s"
- If it's solid wood, add "wood" (not "wooden furniture")
- If it's handmade, add "handmade"
- If the maker or brand is visible, add it: "Stickley," "Arts and Crafts," "Drexel"

**Don't over-tag:**
- One or two material tags (wood + fabric, or ceramic + hand-painted)
- One or two style tags (vintage + mid-century is fine; vintage + modern + cottage + industrial is noise)
- One era tag if applicable
- Brand tag only if the brand is known or relevant

## Common tagging mistakes

**Tagging every possible attribute.** If you add 20 tags, none of them stand out. Focus on the most important 3–5.

**Tagging things that aren't true.** If you tag "solid wood" but it's veneer, shoppers feel deceived. Tags are a promise.

**Forgetting material.** A wooden table needs a "wood" tag. A glass vase needs "glass." Material is the first filter shoppers use.

**Forgetting era or style.** If you have a Victorian chair or a mid-century dresser, tag it. Collectors search by era and style.

**Tagging vaguely.** Don't tag "furniture." That's the category. Use tags for specifics: "dining," "bedroom," "oak," "upholstered."

## Examples by item type

**Wooden dining chair**
- Category: Furniture
- Auto-tags: wood, chair, dining
- Your additions: solid oak, 1950s, vintage (if true)

**Ceramic vase**
- Category: Décor
- Auto-tags: ceramic, vase, blue
- Your additions: hand-painted (if visible), 1960s (if you know), artist name (if known)

**Vintage lamp**
- Category: Lighting
- Auto-tags: lamp, brass, fabric
- Your additions: 1970s, teak (if applicable), table lamp (more specific than "lamp")

**Kitchen mixer**
- Category: Kitchen
- Auto-tags: mixer, appliance, chrome
- Your additions: stand mixer (more specific), vintage KitchenAid (if brand is clear), mint condition (if true)

**Collectible figurine**
- Category: Collectibles
- Auto-tags: figurine, ceramic, collectible
- Your additions: Lladro (if true), mint in box (if true), vintage, hand-painted

## How shoppers filter

On the FindA.Sale search page, shoppers see:

1. **Search box:** "dining chairs" or "vintage lamps"
2. **Category filters:** Furniture, Décor, Electronics, etc.
3. **Tag filters:** Wood, metal, ceramic, vintage, modern, etc.
4. **Condition filters:** Like new, good, fair, poor
5. **Price range slider**

Your tags and category determine whether your item appears in filtered results.

## The big picture

Think like a shopper. If you were looking for something, what would you search for? What tags would you use to find it?

Shoppers searching "oak furniture" will find you if you tag "oak." Shoppers filtering "vintage" will find you if you tag "vintage." The more relevant your tags, the more likely the right shoppers see your listing.
