## Tier 1 — Start This Month (Channels 1–2)

### Channel 1: In-Person GR Estate Sale Circuit (Shopper Track)

**The Play:**  
Patrick attends 3–4 estate sales per Saturday in GR (already doing this for Spike 1 organizer outreach). At each sale, spend 10–15 min talking to **shoppers** while they browse — not organizers. Show the FindA.Sale mobile app on his phone. Collect emails from 5–10 shoppers per sale. Host a "Shopper Friday" weekly email roundup: "Here are 5 sales happening this weekend, with photos/tags from FindA.Sale."

**Week 1 Actions:**
- Create a one-page "Shopper Landing" (not full app signup — just email capture + "Get Friday deals list").
- Set up a simple Google Form or Typeform with: Name, Email, Zip, "Favorite sale type" (estate / yard / flea market).
- Print 50 QR-code business cards pointing to the form (tie physical world to digital).
- Attend 3 sales on Saturday, hand out cards, show app to 15–20 shoppers. Demo tagging 2–3 items on the phone.

**Week 2–4 Actions:**
- Segment emails by zip code and sale type preference.
- Build a Friday weekly email: "5 sales happening near [your zip] this weekend" + item photos/tags from FindA.Sale already-tagged sales.
- Measure: click-through rate on Friday email, sign-up conversion from email to app install.
- If >20% sign up after first Friday email, scale to 2 sales per day on weekends.

**Success Metric:**  
- 50+ emails captured in month 1.
- 15–20% convert to app install.
- Shopper retention: open Friday email >3 consecutive weeks.

**Kill Criterion:**  
- <10 emails in first 2 weeks (suggests no shopper interest).
- Open rate on Friday email <5% after week 3 (suggests list isn't engaged).

**Phase 2 Feasibility:**  
🟢 **HIGH.** Zero cost, overlaps Spike 1 work, uses real data, no tech dependencies, Patrick owns delivery. Risk: shoppers don't trade contact info for a form. Mitigation: make QR card valuable on day 1 (e.g., "Get this weekend's sales in your area" — not a 3-step signup).

### Channel 2: Reddit r/estatesales AMA (Founder)

**The Play:**  
Patrick does a 1-hour Reddit AMA in r/estatesales (8K members, active 3–4x weekly). Title: "I built an app to help you find and track estate sales in real time. I'm giving it away free for the beta. AMA about estate sales, organizing, or what you'd actually use."

*Not a pitch.* A conversation with the community. Answer honest questions about the business (e.g., "How do you make money?" → "Not yet, it's free. We're trying to help."). The implicit value: you'll drop a link in your bio + one reply saying "If you want to try it, here's the link."

**Week 1 Actions:**
- Read r/estatesales for 3–5 days. Identify recurring questions (shopper frustrations, organizer pain points).
- Prepare 15–20 canned responses to likely AMA questions.
- Draft AMA title + opening statement (keep it genuine, not salesy).
- Post AMA. Pin a comment with the app link.

**Week 2–4 Actions:**
- Moderate the AMA live. Answer every question for 2–3 hours.
- Track mentions of pain points. Screenshot the most popular questions — they become feature priorities.
- Monitor signups from Reddit traffic. Measure: Reddit traffic → app install → onboarding completion.
- Share learnings with findasale-product team (what the Reddit community actually wants).

**Success Metric:**
- 50+ upvotes on AMA post.
- 30+ distinct comments.
- 15–25 app installs from Reddit traffic.
- 1–2 serious organizer applications (unlikely but possible).

**Kill Criterion:**
- AMA post stays <10 upvotes after 4 hours (suggests poor title or wrong time).
- <5 comments after 2 hours (suggests low engagement).
- 0 app installs after 24 hours (suggests link not visible or community not interested).

**Phase 2 Feasibility:**  
🟢 **HIGH.** Low cost (Patrick's time only), honest positioning (no overselling), community already wants to talk about estate sales. Risks: (a) Reddit sees self-promotion and downvotes aggressively (mitigated by answering genuine questions first); (b) negative comments about product gaps (mitigation: be honest, say "We're in beta, here's what we don't do yet"). This is soft launch reality-testing, not a scalable channel — treat it like user research.

## Tier 2 — Parallel Channels (Month 2+) — Channels 3–4

### Channel 3: Facebook Organizer Groups

**The Play:**  
Identify 5–10 private FB groups for estate sale organizers, auctioneers, flea market vendors (examples: "Estate Sales Professionals," "Auctioneers Network," "Antique Dealers & Resellers"). **Do NOT post a link.** Instead: answer 2–3 organizer questions per week with genuine advice + 1 line at the end: "We built FindA.Sale to solve [problem you just described] — happy to show you a demo."

Join as Patrick, not a bot account. Real profile, real photo, real engagement.

**Week 1–2:**
- Find and join 8–10 groups via search: "estate sale professionals" + "auction" + "antique dealers" + location variations.
- Lurk for 3–5 days. Identify recurring problems (inventory management, shopper reach, pricing).
- Answer first question. No product mention — pure value.

**Week 3+:**
- Answer 2–3 questions per week across all groups.
- Once or twice per month, when a question directly matches a FindA.Sale feature, mention it: "That's exactly what we built into FindA.Sale. I can show you if you're interested."
- Measure: direct messages asking for a demo, ad-hoc signups.

**Success Metric:**
- 3–5 demo requests per month from FB groups.
- 1–2 signups from organic DMs.

**Kill Criterion:**
- No DM responses after 6 weeks of active answering.
- Group mods flag you as spam or ban you.

**Phase 2 Feasibility:**  
🟡 **MEDIUM.** Highly variable by group — some are open to tool mentions, others are diehard Facebook-only and hostile to SaaS. Requires weekly time commitment. No upfront cost but opportunity cost. **Gated on Spike 1:** If real organizers are on-platform and happy, this channels becomes much more valuable (can share their success stories). If Spike 1 is still soft-launching, this is premature.

### Channel 4: YouTube Shorts (Estate Sale Walkthrough / Time-Lapse Tagging)

**The Play:**  
Create 3–5 short-form videos (15–45 sec):
1. **Estate sale walkthroughs in real time** — Patrick or a real organizer walking a sale, showing items, with FindA.Sale tagging them live. "This bookshelf → $80, marked Furniture. These vintage plates → $15 for 4, marked Kitchenware."
2. **Time-lapse of app tagging** — 2-min estate sale cataloged in 15 seconds. Satisfying to watch. Shows the problem (100 items) and solution (tagged in 2 min).
3. **Shopper perspective** — "How I found $200 in deals this weekend using FindA.Sale."

Upload to YouTube Shorts + TikTok + IG Reels simultaneously (same asset).

**Week 1–2:**
- Film 3 Shorts at upcoming GR estate sales (tie to Spike 1 outreach work).
- Add captions, sound, call-to-action.
- Upload to YouTube Shorts, TikTok, IG Reels.

**Week 3–4:**
- Monitor view counts, likes, shares. Look for one that breaks 1K views — that's your signal to double down on that format.
- Film 2–3 more in the high-performing style.
- Check Analytics: where are clicks coming from? (TikTok, IG, or YouTube?)

**Success Metric:**
- 1 Short breaks 1K views.
- 30+ total app installs from all short-form channels combined.
- Click-through rate to landing page >5%.

**Kill Criterion:**
- After 5 Shorts, no video breaks 500 views (suggests format isn't resonating).
- 0 installs from short-form traffic after 4 weeks (suggests CTA weak or audience wrong).

**Phase 2 Feasibility:**  
🟡 **MEDIUM-HIGH.** Upside is real (one viral short = 500–1K installs). Downside is content hamster wheel — you need a new short every 3–5 days to stay visible. Patrick's comfort level with content creation unknown. Requires real organizer partners to show product in action (gated on Spike 1). Can start without them (just show UI), but authenticity matters for virality.
