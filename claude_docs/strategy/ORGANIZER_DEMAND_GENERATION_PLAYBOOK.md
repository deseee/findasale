# FindA.Sale Organizer Demand Generation Playbook

**Date:** 2026-04-15  
**Status:** Active Playbook  
**Scope:** Acquisition strategy for organizers across all event types (estate sales, yard sales, auctions, flea markets, consignment)  
**Goal:** By the time an organizer talks to Patrick, they're already sold. The conversation is just confirming trial and clarifying details.

---

## Core Principle

**No cold sales.** No email sequences Patrick sends manually to cold lists. No Craigslist scraping. No sales-y tone. Instead: become a trusted community member FIRST, build social proof SECOND, and let organizers come to you warm.

---

## 1. Community Presence Strategy

### Phase 1: Join, Listen, Contribute (Months 1–2)

**Target Communities:**
- **Facebook Groups:** ASEL members + regional estate sale organizer networks (e.g., "Michigan Estate Sale Organizers," "Grand Rapids Business Owners," "Estate Sale Professionals")
- **LinkedIn:** Estate sale company pages, auctioneer networks, secondhand business groups
- **Reddit:** r/Reselling, r/FleaMarkets, r/EstateContents
- **Local:** Grand Rapids Chamber of Commerce, Business Improvement Districts, trade shows (Antiques & Collectibles Fair, estate sale expos)

**Your Role (Patrick or delegated team member):**
- Join as a peer organizer, NOT as a sales rep
- First two weeks: Listen. Read 50+ posts. Learn the language, pain points, recurring questions
- Identify the 2–3 most active groups (by daily engagement and member count)
- Post nothing. React, comment thoughtfully on others' posts

**What to Look For:**
- "How do I price items faster?"
- "Any recommendations for listing software?"
- "Managing inventory manually is killing me"
- "How do you handle payment collection?"
- Photos of messy spreadsheets, listing challenges, workflow struggles

### Phase 2: Become Helpful (Months 1–4, ongoing)

**Post Format & Cadence:**
- **2–3 times per week** in each primary group (not daily — don't spam)
- **Posts must solve a real problem without mentioning FindA.Sale**

**Examples:**

*Post Type 1: Quick Workflow Tip*
"If you're still manually typing descriptions for 50 items, try taking a batch of photos first, writing them all at once (group by category), then uploading. Cuts my data entry time in half. Anyone else have a system that works?"
→ This invites discussion and establishes expertise, not sales

*Post Type 2: Answer a Commonly Asked Question*
Someone posts: "How do you decide starting prices for auctions?"
You reply: "I usually check eBay sold listings for 3 similar items, average the results, then subtract 15% to encourage early bids. Works better than fixed-price IMO. What's your method?"
→ You're teaching, not pitching

*Post Type 3: Poll/Discussion Starter*
"Quick question for the group: how many hours do you spend on photo editing and descriptions per 50-item sale? I'm trying to benchmark what's normal."
→ You're learning, building relationship, positioning as peer

*Post Type 4: Problem You've Solved (Without Naming a Tool)*
"Spent two years exporting my eBay listings manually after each sale. Finally got a workflow where I export straight to CSV, upload to eBay template, done. The time saved has been huge. If anyone wants the CSV template I use, I'll share it."
→ Helpful, specific, positions you as thoughtful and organized

**Forbidden Patterns:**
- "Check out this app I built" — NEVER self-promote in your first 50 posts
- "Here's why you should use [tool]" — sales tone, kills credibility
- "All organizers struggle because..." — positioning yourself as an outsider
- Anything with "AI" in it — reserve this for warm intro

### Phase 3: Earned Credibility (Months 2–4+)

**Signals You've Arrived:**
- People ask you questions in comments ("Hey [your name], how would you approach this?")
- Comments on your posts get 10+ likes before appearing
- People tag you in new posts ("This is what [your name] was talking about")
- You've been asked to share templates, contact info, or advice privately

**When This Happens:**
Move to your bio. Add one sentence: "Running estate sales in [region] since [year]" + your website or contact info. Add a link to your FindA.Sale demo (not a sales page yet — just a working example of the workflow).

**Key Achievement:** You are now a known, trusted voice in the community. Not a salesman. A peer.

## 2. Social Proof Flywheel

### The First 5–10 Organizers Are Your Superpowers

**Goal:** Turn early customers into public advocates who voluntarily tell peers about the product.

### Recruitment Strategy (Not Transactional)

**Find Early Adopters:**
From your community posts, you'll notice: certain people respond thoughtfully, ask follow-ups, seem organized, are trusted by the group. These are candidates. Reach out privately (DM, email, call):

*Warm Intro Script:*
"Hi [name], I've seen you contributing really thoughtfully to the [Facebook group] about workflow efficiency. I've been building a tool specifically for organizers that handles the time-suck parts — photo → auto-tagged listing → ready to publish. It's free to try during beta. Would you be open to trying it with your next sale? I'd love your honest feedback. If it saves you time, great. If it doesn't, that's also valuable."

**NO PITCH. NO PROMISES. Just offering free access + asking for honest feedback.**

### Incentive Structure (Recognition > Discount)

**Do NOT offer 50% off or discounts.** Early customers value recognition, not discount codes. Offer instead:

1. **Public Testimonial (if they like the product)**
   - "Feature [Name]'s Sale" on a FindA.Sale case study page
   - Include: their story (sale type, size, how they used the platform), a quote, a photo of their sale
   - Drive traffic to their FindA.Sale sale page + their own business
   - Link to their business website / social media

2. **Community Badge**
   - "Early Adopter" or "Founding Member" badge in their organizer profile (public, visible on their sale page)
   - Posts a social signal: "This organizer trusted us before we were known"

3. **Private Advisory Access**
   - Monthly 15-minute call with Patrick to discuss features they want
   - "Your voice shaped the product" — genuine, not scripted

4. **Referral Mechanic** (see Section 4 below)
   - When they refer a peer who signs up, they get a real reward (referral credit, extended trial, professional photo editing credit, etc.)

### Conversion Triggers

**When an Early User Loves the Product, Surface It Publicly:**

- Ask them: "Would you be willing to post about this on your [Facebook group]?" (not a sales pitch — genuine feedback)
- They post: "Just used this new tool for my estate sale. Saved me 2 hours on photo descriptions. [Link]"
- This is INFINITELY more credible than Patrick posting

**If They Mention You by Name:**
- Screenshot + thank them publicly (in their comment, or share to FindA.Sale social)
- They feel recognized. Peers see organic enthusiasm, not corporate spin

**Case Study Version (Higher Stakes):**
- 2–3 months in, if user loves the product, ask: "Would you be open to a case study? [Name] uses FindA.Sale for [sale type]. We'd write a 300-word story about your workflow and results, you'd review it, and we'd publish it on our site and share with organizer groups."
- They say yes → you have public proof

## 3. Ringless Voicemail + Content Delivery (Awareness Touch, Not Pitch)

### Current Tactic, Reframed

**Goal:** Voicemail drives to a piece of content that does the selling, not the voicemail itself.

### The Script (15 seconds — informational, not sales-y)

"Hi [name], I'm Patrick from FindA.Sale. I came across [estate sale company / your recent sale]. I built a platform specifically to help organizers like you cut listing time in half using auto-tags and smart pricing. I recorded a 2-minute demo showing the exact workflow. Check it out at [short URL]: finda.sale/demo-[name] — no pressure. If you want to chat after, my number is [your number]. Either way, hope it's helpful."

**Tone:** Peer-to-peer. You found their work, you built something relevant, here's proof it works.

### The Content It Points To

**Option 1: Short Demo Video (2 min)**
- Show a REAL estate sale from your beta user
- Narrate: "This organizer had 50 items. Here's what she did. [Click photo upload]. [Show auto-tagging in action]. [Show pricing suggestion]. [Show export to eBay]. Total time: 22 minutes. Without the tool: 2 hours."
- No music, no hype, no sales language
- End card: "Ready to try it? Click here. Free beta trial, no card needed."
- Host on Loom or YouTube (unlisted, shareable)

**Option 2: Case Study (Written + Photo)**
- Title: "[Organizer Name]'s Estate Sale: 50 Items, 22 Minutes to Listing"
- Three sections: (a) their challenge ("manually entering 50 items was killing me"), (b) how they used the tool (workflow, time saved), (c) their results ("now I can focus on sourcing instead of data entry")
- One quote: their honest take in their words
- 3–4 photos of the sale / their workspace
- CTA: "Try it free"
- Post on Medium or WordPress (SEO-friendly, shareable)

**Option 3: Comparison Chart (Visual)**
- "Estate Sale Listing Workflow: Manual vs. Auto-Tagged"
- Two columns: time, steps, pain points, outcomes
- Makes the value obvious at a glance

### Voicemail Deployment Rules

- **Target 5–10 organizers max per week** (not spray-and-pray)
- **Tier 1:** Professional estate sale companies (1–10 events/month) — they feel the pain most
- **Tier 2:** Auctioneers running regular sales
- **Tier 3:** Consignment shop owners with high turnover
- **Never:** Cold lists from Craigslist or purchased leads. Use your community network instead
- **Timing:** Tuesday–Thursday, 10am–1pm (when they're planning, not in the middle of a sale)
- **Follow-up:** Only if they call back. No annoying sequences. If no call in 2 weeks, stop

## 4. Referral Mechanics (Peer-to-Peer Word of Mouth)

### What Does a Referral Conversation Sound Like?

Organizer A (uses FindA.Sale) talks to Organizer B (doesn't yet) at a trade show or Facebook group.

**Bad referral (doesn't happen):**
A: "You should use FindA.Sale, I get a $50 credit if you sign up"
B thinks: "They're trying to game me for a discount. Skip."

**Good referral (what we want):**
A: "I cut my listing time in half using this tool. It's not perfect, but it's a game-changer if you hate data entry. Want me to send you the link?"
B: "Yeah, sure, maybe"
A: [Shares link]

**The difference:** A is recommending because it genuinely solved a problem, not because there's a kickback.

### Referral Incentive Design (Recognition + Real Value)

**For the Referrer (Person A):**
- **Tier 1 (1–2 referrals/month):** 1 free month of their current plan
- **Tier 2 (3–5 referrals/month):** Professional photo editing credit ($100 value) + public "Ambassador" status on their profile
- **Tier 3 (6+/month):** They're now a paid affiliate — $20/month per active referred customer or 10% of their GMV, whichever is higher

**For the Referee (Person B):**
- **Referred by someone in community:** You acknowledge it. Small email: "Person A recommended us — thanks for the intro. Here's a $15 credit to try it on your next sale."
- NOT "$15 off" — $15 to spend on a paid feature (professional photo editing, advanced pricing tips, premium support chat)
- Creates sense of relationship, not discount-hunting

### Mechanics (How Referral Flow Works)

1. Organizer A logs in → "Invite" or "Refer a Friend" button
2. Generates a personal link: finda.sale/ref/[organizer-a-id]
3. They share via text, email, Facebook post — whatever
4. New organizer signs up through that link
5. B gets $15 credit automatically (no form filling)
6. A gets notified: "[Name] signed up. You're 1/3 toward [next reward]"
7. Tracking is automatic (no manual review needed)

### Affiliate Tier (Scaled Word of Mouth)

**When an organizer hits 6+ referrals/month:**
- Patrick calls them personally
- Offer becomes paid: $20/month per referred customer who stays active (at least 1 sale/month), or 10% of referred customer's GMV, whichever is higher
- They get a custom landing page: finda.sale/[their-name] that includes their testimonial + case study + referral link
- We feature them in the "Partner Organizers" section of the website

**This works because:**
- Organizers who refer that many people genuinely believe in the product
- They have a network worth monetizing
- They feel like they're part of the company, not a salesman

## 5. One Contrarian Idea: Probate Attorney & Estate Planning Consultant Network

### The Insight

When someone dies, their family hires either a probate attorney or an estate liquidation consultant. These professionals KNOW organizers. They recommend them, partner with them, or even refer referrals FROM organizers back to attorneys.

**Reverse flow opportunity:** Attorneys → refer clients with large estates → those clients hire organizers → organizers need FindA.Sale.

### Why This Works

**Probate Attorneys:**
- Client has large estate to liquidate
- Attorney recommends: "You'll need an estate sale organizer. Here are 3 in your area."
- THEN: Organizer struggles with slow listing
- THEN: Organizer discovers FindA.Sale

**Estate Planning Consultants (less common but high-value):**
- High-net-worth clients planning ahead
- Consultant recommends professional estate sale process
- Builds relationships with 2–3 trusted estate sale organizers
- Those organizers use better tools → they take more consignment estates → more referrals back to the consultant

### Execution

**1. Identify 5–10 Probate Attorneys in Your Region (Grand Rapids + Surrounding)**
- Search: "probate attorney Michigan," "estate planning lawyer Grand Rapids"
- Filter for: solo practitioners or 5-person firms (more likely to make referral decisions personally)

**2. Research Their Client Base**
- Look at their website, blog posts — what estate value range do they serve?
- Find one post where they mention estate sale organizers

**3. Warm Introduction** (not cold email)
- Local business network / Chamber of Commerce: "Do you know any probate attorneys? I want to meet them."
- Attend estate-related seminars or workshops — meet them in person
- LinkedIn DM: "I saw your post on estate planning. I'm building a tool for estate sale organizers in [region]. Would love to chat about how we work with attorneys."

**4. In the Conversation (5 min coffee, not a sales pitch)**
"When your clients need to liquidate an estate, they hire an organizer. FindA.Sale saves organizers 2 hours of data entry per sale. Faster turnaround = happier clients = better referral experience for you. I'm not asking for referrals yet. I just want you to know we exist, so if an organizer client asks, you can say, 'I know a tool that might help.' We handle the rest."

**5. Give Them Social Proof Ammunition**
- Send a 1-page PDF: "Estate Sale Organizers: Work Smarter, Not Harder" (not a sales sheet, a helpful guide)
- Include: tips on workflow, case study, your contact info
- They can forward it to organizer clients without looking like they're selling

**6. Mutual Benefit Design**
- Attorney mentions FindA.Sale to their organizer clients
- Organizer finds it valuable
- Organizer THEN refers other organizers in their network (peer-to-peer referral kicks in)
- Patrick sends the attorney a monthly summary: "We're working with [3 organizers you may know]. Thanks for the intro."

### Why It's Contrarian

Most SaaS founders target their direct user (organizer). You're instead building a relationship with the person who has INFLUENCE over organizers WITHOUT competing for their direct business. Probate attorney doesn't use FindA.Sale; they just vouch for it. Low friction, high signal.

## 6. Quarterly Organizer Roundtables (Community Investment)

### Purpose

Not a webinar. Not a pitch. A peer-led discussion where organizers learn from each other and feel part of a community.

### Format (90 min, virtual or in-person)

- **Guest:** Real organizer (not Patrick, not a salesman)
- **Topic:** Something they've mastered (e.g., "Running 3 Sales a Month Without Burning Out," "Pricing Antiques Right the First Time," "Building Team Workflows")
- **Format:** 20 min talk + 40 min Q&A + 30 min hangout (chat or breakout rooms)
- **No CTA.** No "FindA.Sale makes this easier." Just good conversation.

### Promotion

- Announce in the Facebook groups where you've built credibility
- "Hey everyone, [Name] is hosting a discussion on [topic]. She's doing [X] sales/month and sharing her system. Thought this group would find it valuable. [Registration link]"
- Early adopters spread it internally

### Real Value

- You learn what organizers actually struggle with
- Attendees feel heard and find new tactics
- Word of mouth: "That roundtable was actually useful, not a pitch"
- Post-roundtable email (with permission) is warm, not cold

## 8. What NOT to Do

- **No cold email sequences** — Patrick doesn't send 50 emails to cold lists
- **No Craigslist scraping** — builds terrible list quality, attracts low-commitment leads
- **No paid ads to organizers yet** — wait until you have 20+ true advocates, then use them in ad creative
- **No "AI" in any copy** — use "Auto-tags," "Smart Pricing," or just "We tag it for you"
- **No discount-first incentives** — never lead with "20% off." Lead with "saves 2 hours."
- **No spray-and-pray voicemails** — voicemail only to people you've researched and who are likely to care
- **No removal of agency** — early adopters should feel like partners, not leads. Ask them things, listen, adjust
- **No transactional relationships** — if the only reason they know you is because you want them to buy, you've failed this strategy

## 9. Roadmap: 90-Day Rollout

### Month 1 (Weeks 1–4)
- Join 3 primary Facebook groups + LinkedIn
- Post nothing yet; read 50+ existing posts
- Identify 2–3 most active groups
- Identify 5–10 candidate organizers for early access

### Month 1–2 (Weeks 5–8)
- Start posting 2x/week in primary groups (helpful, no mention of product)
- Reach out privately to 3 organizers with voicemail + demo link
- Prepare short demo video + case study doc
- Create referral mechanics (landing page, email template, tracking)

### Month 2–3 (Weeks 9–12)
- Scale posts to 2–3x/week across groups
- Voicemail campaign: 2–3 organizers per week (15–20 total)
- First organizers signing up + providing feedback
- Prepare case studies of top early users
- Identify 5 local probate attorneys

### Month 3 (Weeks 13–16)
- Begin attorney outreach (warm intros from network)
- First roundtable discussion (on a topic your early organizers suggest)
- First case study published + shared widely
- Referral loop should be active (some early customers referring peers)
- Measure: 10–15 warm signups, 5+ real organizers in trial/paid
